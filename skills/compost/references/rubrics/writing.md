# Rubric - writing

Use for drafts, essays, newsletters, docs, scripts, or long notes (`writing` primary or secondary).

## Completeness checkpoints (0-100%)

| % | Checkpoint | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Topic named | title or first line states the subject |
| 25 | Claim stated | a thesis or a promise to the reader exists |
| 40 | Outline | sections or bullets cover the arc |
| 60 | Full draft | every section has prose |
| 75 | Edited | second-pass edits, cuts, consistent voice |
| 90 | Published | URL, newsletter issue, post |
| 100 | Responded to | comments, replies, shares, subscribers |

## Where parts hide

- `sentence`: a line that states something sharp and specific (cite paragraph number). Hooks, definitions, analogies.
- `problem`: a precisely described pain with who has it.
- `data`: numbers, tables, interview quotes, collected examples.
- `design`: a structure that worked (series format, recurring section).

## Stop signals

| Signal | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Outline done, prose stops at section 2-3 | `unfinished` |
| Published, no audience growth noted | `no-revenue` (if meant to earn) or `other` |
| Several restarts with different intros | `abandoned` or `other` (unclear claim) |
| Claim changes between sections | `other` (thesis unclear) |

## Pitfalls

- Evidence locations for writing are `<artifact-id>:<file>:¶<n>` (paragraph number) or a heading anchor.
- Do not grade style. Lessons are about why it stopped, not about prose quality.
